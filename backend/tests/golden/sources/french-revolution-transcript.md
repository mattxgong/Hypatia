# Lecture 2: The French Revolution

[00:00] Welcome back everyone. Today we're discussing the French Revolution, one of the
most transformative events in modern history.

[01:15] The Revolution began in 1789 with the Storming of the Bastille on July 14th.
The causes were complex: economic crisis, food shortages, and anger at royal extravagance
under King Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette.

[03:45] The Estates-General was convened in May 1789, bringing together representatives
of the three estates: clergy, nobility, and commoners. The Third Estate, representing
commoners, declared itself the National Assembly on June 17th.

[06:20] Key phases of the Revolution:
1. The Constitutional Monarchy phase (1789-1792)
2. The Radical Republic and Reign of Terror (1792-1794)
3. The Directory and rise of Napoleon (1795-1799)

[08:30] Maximilien Robespierre led the Committee of Public Safety during the Reign of
Terror. Between 16,000 and 40,000 people were executed, mostly by guillotine. Robespierre
himself was executed in July 1794, ending the Terror.

[11:00] The Revolution produced the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen,
which proclaimed liberty, equality, and popular sovereignty. These ideas influenced
democratic movements worldwide.

[13:15] Napoleon Bonaparte seized power in 1799 through a coup d'état, establishing
the Consulate and later the First French Empire. He spread Revolutionary ideals across
Europe through military conquest.

[15:00] Key legacy: abolition of feudalism, separation of church and state, the
Napoleonic Code, and the modern concept of citizenship. The Revolution fundamentally
changed the relationship between people and government.
